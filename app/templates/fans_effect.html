How this is measured

  • A switch: a building's fans going from mostly on to mostly off (or the reverse), having been the other way for the hour before, and staying the new way for the next {{ window_h }} hours. A switch may ramp over up to 20 minutes as groups start or stop one after another.
  • What is measured: how far the apartment average (the building's 2010 box) moved from the hour before the switch to the last hour of the window, and how many minutes the burner ran in the window.
  • Compared with: the same building, same time of day (±20 min), on other days within {{ control_days }} days either side when the fans stayed as they were, and it was within {{ control_out }}° outside. The effect is the switch minus the average of those days, so the time of day, the weather and the building's own habits cancel out.
  • Why the burner matters as much as the temperature: when the fans pull heat out, the box sees cooler apartments and runs the burner longer, so much of the fans' cost shows up as fuel rather than as colder apartments.
  • The ± figures are 95% margins. An effect larger than its margin is real; a smaller one may be chance. Apartment readings are whole degrees, so a few tenths of a degree only show up averaged over many switches.
